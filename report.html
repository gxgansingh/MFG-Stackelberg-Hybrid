Network-1 Ablation Study

Experimental design

  • Allocation methods: A1 MFG only, A2 Stackelberg only, A3 MFG + Stackelberg, and Proposed priority-aware MFG + Stackelberg.
  • Load levels: 1250, 2500, 5000.
  • Priority profiles: T1, T2, T3.
  • Replication labels: 11, 22, 33, 44, 55.
  • Total runs: 180.
  • Matched conditions: load, priority profile, and replication seed.
  • Each method uses the same generated workload and a fresh environment initialized with the same seed within each matched condition.
  • Resource feasibility filtering is shared across all methods.

Two-sided paired t-tests are used, with Holm correction across the family of 18 tests (six metrics and three baselines). Confidence intervals are 95% intervals for the mean paired difference.

The gap and margin values from the current game implementations are candidate-ranking diagnostics; zero gaps alone do not establish a full Nash or Stackelberg equilibrium.

Interpretation cautions

  • Overall means combine multiple load levels and priority profiles and are descriptive, not a substitute for condition-specific results.
  • A statistically significant difference does not by itself establish practical importance or universal superiority.
  • Paired tests assume the matched differences are sufficiently well behaved for a t-test. The 45 matched conditions include repeated profiles and loads, so the pooled test should be interpreted alongside condition-level results.
  • Jain fairness measures fairness of task allocation counts across nodes, not fairness of completed work, resource consumption, or task outcomes.
  • Throughput is completed tasks divided by the configured duration in ticks.
  • Allocation computation time is measured around the complete policy decision. It is not the same as MFG convergence time.
